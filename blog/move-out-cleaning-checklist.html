Renter's Checklist

The Complete Move-Out
Cleaning Checklist for WA Renters

Everything your landlord or property manager will check before returning your deposit — organized room by room so nothing gets missed.

Move-In / Move-Out·Washington State·7 min read

Under Washington's Residential Landlord-Tenant Act (RCW 59.18), a landlord can only withhold your security deposit for damage beyond normal wear and tear — but "the unit wasn't clean" is one of the most common reasons deposits get partially withheld anyway. The frustrating part is that most move-out disputes come down to a handful of spots people simply forget. Here's the checklist that covers what actually gets inspected.

Before you start: Take dated photos of every room once cleaning is complete. If there's ever a dispute over your deposit, this is your best evidence that the unit was left in good condition.

Kitchen

  • Inside and outside of the oven, including racks — this is the #1 spot inspectors flag
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer (unplugged and doors propped open after, if vacant)
  • Inside and outside of the microwave
  • All cabinet interiors and drawers, including crumbs in tracks
  • Countertops, backsplash, and sink — including under the sink
  • Stovetop and range hood/filter
  • Floors, including behind and under appliances if movable

Bathrooms

  • Toilet — including the base, hinges, and behind the tank
  • Tub and shower, including grout and any soap scum buildup
  • Sink, faucet, and mirror (watch for water spots)
  • Inside medicine cabinets and vanity drawers
  • Exhaust fan cover — often overlooked and visibly dusty
  • Floors and baseboards

Bedrooms & Living Areas

  • All closet interiors, shelving, and rods
  • Baseboards and window sills
  • Light fixtures and ceiling fan blades
  • Interior windows and tracks
  • Vacuum carpets thoroughly; mop or sweep hard floors
  • Wipe down light switch plates and door handles

Whole-Unit Details People Forget

  • Walls — spot clean scuffs and marks, especially near light switches
  • Air vents and return registers
  • Inside washer and dryer, if included in the unit
  • Garage or storage unit, if applicable
  • Patio, balcony, or entryway
  • Remove all trash — including bathroom and bedroom cans

How Long Does This Actually Take?

For a typical one-bedroom apartment, plan on 3–5 hours if you're doing it solo and the unit has been reasonably maintained. A neglected two- or three-bedroom home can take a full day. This is exactly why so many renters end up booking a professional move-out clean — between packing, coordinating the move itself, and a full-time job, a multi-hour deep clean is often the first thing that gets rushed.

Final Tip

Do your move-out clean after all furniture and belongings are out of the unit — cleaning around boxes and furniture means you'll miss spots that get exposed the moment everything's gone. Schedule your final clean for the same day as your move-out walkthrough whenever possible, so the unit is at its cleanest exactly when it's being inspected.
